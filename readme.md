# [Channel Estimation + Positioning] A Novel Distributed, Phase-II Offset Matched Filtering Algorithm for Millimeter Wave MIMO Channel Estimation with MATLAB Code

Introduction: Due to the emergence of high-definition communication, immersive experiences, and industrial automation among new services, the next generation network's demand for high data rates and low latency communication will continue to increase. Consequently, shorter wavelengths at millimeter wave (mmWave) or terahertz frequencies will be utilized in multi-input multi-output (MIMO) systems in the past and future. However, increasing frequency also reduces the propagation capability of waves. This results in significant path loss at the user equipment (UE), necessitating advanced methods such as directional beamforming to compensate for its drawbacks. To support beamforming, sparse channel estimation algorithms have been introduced [2]. For example, they are Orthogonal Matching Pursuit (OMP) [1], Sparse Bayesian Learning (SBL) [9] and Approximate Message Passing (AMP) algorithms [6] [10].
Although the benefits of sparse methods have been proven in many previous works, there is still a need for more work to surpass the current level of technology in mmWave channel estimation using sparse methods. There are two weaknesses in the current research on sparse channel estimation: i) The number of paths, that is, the sparsity level, is usually assumed to be sparse in the angle domain for mmWave channels, where the number of paths is. These assumptions reduce the practical significance of sparse estimation methods. First, estimating the number of paths in the channel is not a simple task. Second, the nature of mmWave channels only approximates to sparseness in the sense that there exists a set of non-zero terms with small amplitudes concentrated around the main (highest) one [5].
This paper proposes a novel distributed hierarchical orthogonal matching pursuit (DStOMP)† algorithm for the estimation of millimeter-wave MIMO channels. Our method leverages the distributed setting by estimating the channel using multiple subcarriers instead of individual carriers. It is based on the fact that in terms of noise robustness, multiple measurement vector settings always outperform single measurement vector settings. The StOMP [1] algorithm was chosen as the primary algorithm for two practical reasons. Firstly, it does not require prior knowledge at the sparse level. Secondly, it is particularly well-suited to millimeter-wave channels, which have specific structures. Leveraging the special structure of the DStOMP and the channel, we introduce a new clustering algorithm capable of identifying the number of paths and determining under what dominant support condition the channel is considered sparse.

Conclusion In this study, we propose a new method for MIMO millimeter-wave channel estimation with a hybrid analog-digital architecture. Compared to earlier works, our method offers a practical solution, the Distributed StOMP algorithm. To reduce the ambiguity of the DStOMP algorithm, we introduce a new algorithm that estimates the number of paths using a modified shadowing method in the channel. Simulation results show that our proposed method, without prior knowledge of signal sparsity, can overcome the standard OMP method and achieve nearly the same performance as the SOMP method.
